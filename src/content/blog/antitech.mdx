thoughts about the invasion and integration of technology, specifically of the phone variety, into our daily lives from a guy that works in tech. the following will largely be structureless and incoherent, and largely informed by my personal relationship with technology, which transparently comes from a position of some privledge.

It is undoubtly true that improvements in smartphone technology has enabled and increased level of safety and interconnectedness. Satillite calling, a relatively up-to-date atlas, and notification systems that can notify when something adverse might have happened to your loved ones, all in your palm of your hand, is an incredible feat. 

However, I feel that as I've grown up, I've witnessed digital technology grow from something that was created to augment and supplement the human experience to something to commertialise and commoditise it. Every "Ask app not to track" notification is a subtle reminder that in a land of free applications and social medias, our behaviours, preferences, and urges are the product. That big tech takes this data and turns it right back on us, subliminally influencing us on every level. It promotes this ideal of limitless consumerism, hyper-personalisation, and dangerous herd mentalism designed to remove critical thinking and self-control from the equation. It removes intentionality.

In retrospect, for much of my time in university, I had an extremely poor relationship with technology, falling victim to the ever prevalent passtime of doomscrolling. Why engage in my hobbies when my phone can deliver me curated content straight to my face in the comfort of my bed? Why try new things and get out of my shell when I could instead lie in the soft, welcoming embrace of the algorithm and get shown exactly what I want? Why face intellectual conflict when I could simply exist in an echo chamber the size of my palm? In the years where I, for one of the last times in my life, had a safety net, giving me the privledge of trying something new with a cushion to catch me if I fall hard, I instead fell for the siren song of the algorithm. I regret it deeply, and resultingly hold a minor grudge against the current state of technology. 

More wholly, it fosters a culture where mindless sendintarism is accepted as an appropriate substitution for intentional rest, where a constant stream of manufactured dopamine is treated equivalently to true satisfation and happiness, and where a yearning for adversity and challenge is replaced with complacency.

I'm not saying social media should be completely removed from our lives; I feel that we should have better control over it. It should not be that we fight for control over our technology, but rather that we naturally feel we are in control. (yadda yadda, everything in moderation, yadda yadda).

Outside of the issue of the algorithm, I am also mildly troubled by how interconnected today's phones allow us to be with others. I don't deny that it can be helpful from a safety standpoint, or convienent for when I'm bored and want to call a friend when I'm out and about. My issue arises that the *ability* to be easily reachable has over the years turned into the *expectation* that you must be reachable at all time. As someone that likes my alone time, the fact that the default for phones is to be notified for every single little thing is bothering. Taking this idea to its extreme, it might be said that the normalisation of phone notifications is actively makes society more dangerous as genuinely important notifications will get lost to a sea of other notifications. 

In line with my disdain for a large presence of tech in my daily, non-work life, it frustates me to no end that it seems every phone manufacture has decided that the phablet is the new phone. I want something that comfortably fits in my phone, not a massive fucking brick that challenges the integrity of my Uniqlo shorts pocket seams. I jest, but seriously, I think the iPhone 12/13 mini was the perfect form factor a phone. Large enough to make calls, respond to messages, and search stuff up, but small enough to seamlessly to disappear into my pocket. An alternative argument for my preference for a small form factor phone: if you strip down a phone into only its useful/safety features, and there's no point in it being big.

Right now, I have two phones: an iPhone 13 and an iPhone 7 plus that used to be my mother's. The 13 is my main phone; it has a cellular plan, my main messaging apps, travel apps, banking apps, etc. It also has a sideloaded version of YouTube with shorts completely disabled. The 7 is my social media; it hosts Instagram, Snapchat, unadulterated YouTube, etc. I implemented this separation in university when I was extremely addicted to technology and social media, and although I feel that I have a handled and quelled the urge to be chronically connected, I don't feel the need to remerge social media apps back into my main phone. On my 13, I also have the greyscale colour filter on at almost all times. I have the filter on roughly 80% intensity- I acknowledge that colour is a key consideration in UI/UX; 80% intensity is low enough where I can make out the green of the confirm button or the red of the cancel button, but strong enough to help reduce the urge to use my phone. I also have an automation set up to disable the filter when entering the camera or photo app, and back on when I exit those apps. This works reasonably well. Unfortunately, the newer iOS updates have not been kind to the 13s performance, so much to my dismay, I have been considering upgrading.

# New Phones

The simple choice is to just buy an iPhone 17. This sucks, because ideally, I want all of this colour filters, anti-phone addiction stuff treated as a part of the phone's design, not included as a niche feature. Also, it's expensive.

## Minimalist Phones
As a result, I've done some research into minimalist phone options. My ideal phone is as follows:
- Small form factor
- Makes calls, texts, etc.
- Can download apps. Apps like WeChat and Discord are important for communicating with my friends and family.
- Navigation
- NFC Tap to Pay
- Bluetooth
- Preferably e-ink.
- Preferably has a camera
- Preferably satillite calling

Pretty much, I want all the new safety features with none of the bloat.
